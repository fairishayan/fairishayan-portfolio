Smart Classroom and Timetable Scheduler

The Real-World Problem

In rural India, more than 9,000 schools operate with only a single teacher managing multiple primary and middle grade levels simultaneously. In this multi-grade environment, classical static timetables result in idle student cohorts, unstructured teaching hours, and high teacher burnout. The challenge was compounded by limited or intermittent internet connectivity in remote villages.

The Algorithmic Approach

Conceived a mathematical "Focused Rotation" schedule framework. Rather than forcing one teacher to lecture simultaneously to disparate grades, the school day is divided into rotating instructional, peer-study, and self-directed activity blocks. The scheduling model was formulated as a Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) balancing:

  • Hard Constraints: No teacher double-booking, max classroom occupancy, mandatory curriculum coverage per grade level.
  • Soft Constraints: Balanced subject distribution across weekdays, optimal placement of high-focus subjects (Mathematics, Science) in morning blocks, and minimized student context switching.

Technical Implementation

Developed in Python leveraging Google OR-Tools CP-SAT solver. Modeled integer decision variables for teacher assignments, room allocations, and slot matrices. Implemented custom scoring penalties for soft constraint violations. Designed an offline-first data layer allowing schedules to be computed or loaded locally without requiring active network connectivity.

Current Status & Research Outcome

The project was built for the Smart India Hackathon (SIH), reaching approximately 90% completion. The core constraint solver and scheduling algorithms were validated against simulated school profiles. A comprehensive research paper draft documenting the scheduling model and comparative efficiency metrics is currently in progress.

Behavioral E-Learning Management System (ELMS)

The Real-World Problem

Mainstream learning platforms suffer from low course completion rates and superficial passive consumption. Students often watch video modules or click through slides without active cognitive retention or behavioral accountability, leading to poor conceptual retention.

The Behavioral Framework: "Truth or Dare"

Pioneered an active verification loop coined "Truth or Dare":

  • Truth Modules: Evaluative conceptual challenges where students must explain, verify, or dissect learned concepts rather than picking multiple-choice guesses.
  • Dare Modules: Practical applied mini-tasks (writing code snippets, solving targeted algorithmic edge cases, or formulating structured explanations) that require proof of mastery.

Technical Implementation

Constructed full-stack on the MERN stack (MongoDB, Express.js, React, Node.js). Embedded a Python NLP backend using tokenization, intent recognition, and similarity heuristics to provide automated contextual grading and conversational chatbot assistance. Student engagement metrics, recall latency, and verification states were structured in MongoDB.

Current Status & Research Outcome

Successfully completed as the final-year BCA project at Integral University. Synthesized the findings into a complete research paper articulating the behavioral verification framework, currently drafted and awaiting publication evaluation.

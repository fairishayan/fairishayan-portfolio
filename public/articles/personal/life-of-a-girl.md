# What I Think About the Life of a Girl and Why

I think a girl's life can depend on many factors: her family background, her parents' beliefs, the society around her, her workplace, her geographical location, her education, her financial independence, and, in some situations, even her appearance and body.

Gender is only one part of the equation. The environment in which a girl grows up can completely change what kind of life she is able to live.

I have already discussed the difficulties women can face in my chapter, "Do Men Have a Harder Life Than Women?" In this chapter, I want to look at the same subject from a different perspective: how different social and family conditions can shape a girl's actual life.

## A Girl Growing Up in a Village

Consider a girl growing up in a socially conservative rural environment.

Her opportunities can be heavily influenced by the beliefs of her family and community. In some parts of rural India, especially where deeply traditional gender roles remain strong, girls may face restrictions on education, mobility, friendships, clothing, relationships, employment, and marriage choices.

In the most extreme situations, girls may be treated less like independent individuals and more like future wives and caregivers.

They may be expected to learn household responsibilities from childhood, remain close to home, avoid certain friendships, limit their interactions with boys, and eventually marry according to their family's decision.

Child marriage is another serious problem in some communities, even though it is illegal in India. A girl who is married before she has had the opportunity to complete her education or develop financial independence can enter adulthood with very limited control over her own life.

This is where geography becomes important.

A girl growing up in a highly conservative village may technically have the same legal rights as a girl living in a major city, but having a right and being able to exercise that right are two different things.

## The Obsession With Female Sexuality

One of the things I find particularly disturbing is the way a woman's character can sometimes be judged through her sexuality.

There are communities where myths surrounding virginity and the hymen still influence how a woman's character is judged.

One particularly dangerous misconception is that bleeding during first intercourse proves virginity.

It does not.

The hymen is a thin and variable piece of tissue, and its appearance or condition cannot reliably determine whether someone has had sex. It can naturally vary from person to person, and changes to it can occur for reasons unrelated to sexual activity.

Therefore, the idea that a woman must bleed during her first sexual experience is medically unreliable.

Yet this misconception can have serious consequences when families or communities treat it as proof of a woman's character.

I have heard accounts of situations in which newly married women were publicly questioned, humiliated, pressured, or even subjected to violence because they did not bleed after first intercourse. Some alleged practices described in parts of Rajasthan and elsewhere involve so-called tests of virginity or community-level judgments about a woman's sexual history.

Whether every individual story is accurate is something that must be investigated case by case. But the underlying problem is real: a woman's dignity and character should never be determined by a supposed physical "test."

The frightening part is that when such beliefs become socially accepted, a woman may have very little ability to defend herself against them.

## The Difference Between a Village and a City

The situation can change significantly when we move from a highly conservative rural environment to a small or medium-sized city.

Girls in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities may have greater access to education, employment, transportation, technology, and social interaction. Many families actively support their daughters' education and careers.

But greater opportunity does not automatically mean complete freedom.

A girl may be allowed to study but not choose her career.

She may be allowed to work but expected to return home early.

She may be allowed to have friends but not male friends.

She may be allowed to use a smartphone but have her messages monitored.

She may be encouraged to become financially independent but still be expected to perform most household responsibilities.

And eventually, marriage may become the next major expectation.

In some families, a daughter can spend twenty years being told to become educated and independent, only to discover that independence has an expiration date.

## Tier 1 Cities

In large metropolitan cities, the situation can be very different.

Many girls have considerably more freedom to study, work, travel, make friends, use technology, live independently, and make decisions about relationships.

Some families give their daughters substantial autonomy after adulthood. They may accept that their daughter can choose her own career, relationship, or partner.

I do not think this approach is inherently wrong.

In fact, I think there is something valuable in the idea that parents should guide a child during childhood and gradually give them greater control over their own life as they become adults.

But even in major cities, women do not necessarily experience the same freedom as men.

The biggest difference is often safety.

A woman may be legally free to go somewhere, but still have to think about whether it is safe to travel there alone.

She may think about the time of day, transportation, the people around her, whether she should share her location, whether someone is following her, or whether she can safely return home.

Freedom on paper and freedom that feels safe are not always the same thing.

## Appearance Can Also Influence a Girl's Life

Another uncomfortable factor is appearance.

Society can treat attractive women differently from women who do not fit conventional beauty standards. Physical appearance can influence attention, social treatment, relationships, workplace perceptions, and even marriage prospects.

This does not mean appearance determines a woman's entire life. Education, personality, family, financial independence, social environment, and individual decisions can be far more important.

But pretending appearance has no influence at all would also be unrealistic.

Human beings judge each other visually, sometimes consciously and sometimes unconsciously.

The problem begins when a woman's value becomes excessively tied to how she looks.

## The Biggest Difference Is Not Geography Alone

I do not think we can simply divide women into "village girls" and "city girls."

Two girls living in the same city can have completely different lives.

One may have parents who encourage her to study abroad, choose her career, and marry whenever she wants.

Another may have parents who control her clothing, friendships, education, phone, career, and marriage.

The same geographical location can therefore produce completely different experiences.

This is why I think family mentality is one of the strongest factors.

A supportive family can give a girl opportunities even in a conservative environment.

A controlling family can restrict a girl even in a modern city.

Education and financial independence then become extremely important because they increase a person's ability to make decisions independently.

## What I Think a Girl Should Be Able to Control

For me, a girl should eventually have meaningful control over the major decisions of her own life.

She should be able to decide what she wants to study.

She should be able to develop a career if she wants one.

She should be able to decide whether she wants to work or not.

She should be able to choose whether and when she wants to marry.

She should be able to participate in choosing her life partner.

She should be able to reject a relationship without being punished for it.

She should be able to say no to physical intimacy.

She should be able to live without constantly having her character questioned because of her clothes, friendships, relationships, or sexuality.

And most importantly, she should be treated as a person before being treated as someone's daughter, wife, daughter-in-law, or mother.

## What I Think Overall

The life of a girl is not determined by one factor.

It is the result of several systems interacting with each other: family, culture, geography, education, economic independence, social expectations, safety, and individual personality.

A girl growing up in a highly restrictive environment may have very little control over her own life. Another girl growing up in a supportive metropolitan family may experience enormous freedom.

That difference is why I do not think the question should simply be, "Do girls have freedom?"

The better question is:

"How much control does this particular girl actually have over her own life?"

Because legal rights, social freedom, family permission, financial independence, and personal safety are not the same thing.

A woman can technically be free and still feel controlled.

Another woman can live within a traditional family and still have genuine autonomy.

For me, the ideal situation is not a world where women are given special treatment. It is a world where being born a girl does not automatically reduce the number of choices available to her.

She should have the opportunity to become educated, financially independent, emotionally secure, socially respected, and capable of choosing the direction of her own life.

That, in my view, is what genuine freedom should mean.

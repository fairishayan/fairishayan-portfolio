# What Success Means to Me and Why

In the simplest terms, success for me means becoming a good husband, a good father, and a good son-in-law.

That sounds simple.

It isn't.

Behind that definition are several conditions that determine whether this idea of success will actually be possible. For me, success is not simply about having a family. It is about reaching a stage in life where I have enough capability, financial stability, freedom, and control over my time to genuinely be present for that family.

And that is where my definition of success becomes complicated.

## Before Family Comes Preparation

Before getting married, I want to become financially and professionally established, or at least be very close to it.

My first priority until then will be my education, research, skills, and portfolio. I want to spend my most productive years becoming as capable as possible.

I don't want to enter marriage while still struggling to establish myself.

My personal financial benchmark for marriage is an annual income of at least ₹50 lakh. If I am significantly below that level, I would find it difficult to consider myself financially prepared for marriage.

But even ₹50 lakh is not the ultimate goal.

For me, income should be a consequence of capability rather than the primary objective.

I want to reach a level where I can contribute to serious AI research, develop new algorithms, work on difficult computational problems, explore Artificial General Intelligence, and potentially combine AI with fields such as biotechnology.

If I eventually reach that level of capability, my long-term financial expectations naturally become much higher.

But there is another condition that matters just as much as income:

**Time.**

I don't want a career where I earn an enormous amount of money but have no life outside work.

Ideally, I want to work around four days a week, roughly four or five hours a day. I know that sounds extremely ambitious, but that is the kind of professional life I want to build toward.

I want to put most of my intense effort into my education and research before I have a family. I am willing to spend years pursuing an MCA, M.Tech, PhD, research projects, and whatever else is necessary to become highly capable.

But once I have a family, my priorities will change.

My goal is not to spend my entire life working so that my family can have a comfortable life while barely seeing me.

I want to actually be there.

## Why Family Comes First

I have experienced loneliness.

I know what it feels like to want someone's presence and not have it.

Because of that, I don't want my future wife or children to grow up feeling that they have a husband or father who provides everything except his time.

If my career ever reaches a point where it consistently prevents me from being present for my family, I would seriously consider changing or even leaving that career.

Money can be earned again.

Time cannot.

That is why I want to become a **family-first researcher**.

I don't want research and family to compete with each other. I want to build my career in a way that eventually gives me enough freedom to have both.

## Why I Want to Be a Good Husband

Being a good husband would be my highest priority.

My wife would be the person who deliberately chose me as her life partner. My parents didn't choose me as their son, my siblings didn't choose me as their brother, and my future children won't choose me as their father.

Those relationships exist because of circumstances and family bonds.

A marriage is different.

My wife would have the freedom to choose whom she wants to build her life with.

That choice carries enormous meaning for me.

If someone voluntarily chooses to share their life with me, I want to make sure that choosing me never becomes something they regret.

I want to be someone with whom she feels emotionally safe, respected, understood, and valued.

I don't want our relationship to survive merely because we became husband and wife.

I want it to remain alive because we continue choosing each other.

Even after many years, I want us to maintain affection, companionship, intimacy, humour, and shared experiences. I don't want marriage to become nothing more than two people managing a household together.

I want us to remain a couple.

## Why I Want to Be a Good Son-in-Law

My expectations from my own parents and my expectations from my future in-laws are different.

I don't particularly care about proving myself to people who already believe that I am incapable or that my education is a waste of money. I don't want to spend my life trying to convince people who have already decided what they think about me.

But my wife's parents would be different.

If they trust me enough to let me become part of their daughter's life, I would want to treat them with the same respect I would want someone to show my own parents.

I would want them to feel that they gained a son rather than simply losing a daughter.

If they ever need me, I want to be there.

If they need help with their grandchildren, I want to help.

And if they have beliefs or opinions different from mine, I would still want to respect them as individuals while maintaining my own principles.

One thing I feel particularly strongly about is how children should be raised.

I want my children to become human beings before they become representatives of any particular ideology or religion.

I want them to learn how to think, question, observe, understand nature, respect other people, and develop their own worldview.

If religion eventually becomes meaningful to them, I would rather they explore it consciously than inherit it without questioning.

## Why I Want to Be a Good Father

Being a father is probably the role I think about most deeply.

I don't want to be only a parent.

I want to be a mentor, a friend, and a father at the same time.

There are things I have experienced in my own life that I don't want my children to experience in the same way.

I want to take the things I learned from my father, improve upon the things I believe could have been better, and give my children the best version of what I learned.

I want to be present during their successes and failures.

I want them to be able to tell me about their problems without first thinking:

**"If I tell Dad, he will get angry."**

I want them to think:

**"If I tell Dad, he will help me understand what to do."**

That difference matters enormously.

I believe children are shaped not only by what parents teach them, but by how present those parents are.

If a child is an artwork, then parental presence, attention, maturity, understanding, and guidance are some of the tools used to create it.

The quality of those tools can influence how that child grows, learns, thinks, and eventually understands the world.

## How I Will Know I Succeeded

My definition of success is not a number in a bank account.

There are three moments that would mean much more to me.

The first would be seeing my wife look at another couple laughing together or simply enjoying each other's company and thinking:

**"I know they aren't happier than we are."**

If she genuinely feels that way about our relationship, I would consider myself 50% successful.

The second would be the day my children come to me with serious problems without being afraid of my reaction.

If they can sit with me, tell me what went wrong, ask for advice, admit their mistakes, and trust that I will help them rather than simply punish them, I would consider myself 75% successful.

And the third would be the day my parents-in-law genuinely feel:

**"He is like our own son, perhaps even more than we ever expected."**

If I reach that point, I would feel that I have achieved almost everything I wanted from life.

But even then, my responsibilities would not be finished.

## Growing Old Together

I don't want my relationship with my wife to end emotionally once we become parents.

We would still need to make time for each other.

We would still need to laugh together, travel together, create memories, have private moments, and continue discovering each other as we grow older.

Eventually, our children would have their own lives.

Our careers would slow down.

Our responsibilities would become lighter.

And perhaps we would spend more time travelling, revisiting places we once dreamed about, and sitting together remembering everything we built.

Old age will probably bring its own difficulties.

Our health will change.

Our energy will decrease.

Some people we love will no longer be around.

There will probably be painful moments.

But I want those years to be filled with memories rather than regret.

I want to look back and know that I didn't spend my entire life chasing money, status, or professional recognition while missing the people I worked so hard for.

That is why my definition of success is ultimately very simple.

I want to become capable enough to build the life I want.

I want to become financially independent enough to protect it.

I want enough professional freedom to actually live it.

And once I have achieved those things, I want to spend the rest of my life being a good husband, a good father, and a good son-in-law.

**For me, success is not about having the most impressive life.

It is about having a life I would never want to exchange for someone else's.**
